  #headline
  The Contract

  #title
  One contract. :br Both ends

  #description
  One source of truth for both sides of the wire. Describe your app once on the server, and the client is typed from it automatically — every event, request, channel, and stream, flipped to the client's point of view. Drift between them becomes a red squiggle in your editor, not a 2 a.m. incident.
  :::

  :::features-feature-channels
  ---
  link: /learn/channels
  link-label: How channels work
  ---
  #headline
  Channels

  #title
  Rooms, scoped :br to their members

  #description
  Rooms with membership that actually means something. You decide who gets in, and a broadcast reaches exactly the people inside — the rules live on your server, never shipped to the client to bypass. Presence, online/offline, and dynamic room families come in the box.
  :::

  :::features-feature-streaming
  ---
  link: /learn/streaming
  link-label: How streaming works
  ---
  #headline
  Streaming

  #title
  Push data :br as it's made

  #description
  Send data to the client the moment it exists — token by token, event by event, each chunk typed end to end. Built for LLM output and live feeds, with nudges to steer a run while it's still going and durable resume that picks up right where a dropped connection left off.
  :::

  :::features-feature-requests
  ---
  link: /learn/requests
  link-label: How requests work
  ---
  #headline
  Requests

  #title
  Ask, and :br await the answer

  #description
  Real request/response, over a WebSocket. Ask the server a question and `await` a typed reply — no event wiring, no correlation IDs to track by hand. Fire as many as you like on one connection and each answer comes back to the caller that asked, even out of order. And it works both ways: the server can ask the client too.
  :::

  :::features-feature-reliability
  ---
  link: /learn/reliability
  link-label: How reliability works
  ---
  #headline
  Reliability

  #title
  Drops happen. :br Nothing gets lost

  #description
  Real networks drop sockets — a sleeping laptop, a flaky tower, a deploy mid-session. WSKit keeps the connection healthy and your data consistent through all of it: spot a dead socket, reconnect with backoff, hold the sends you made while offline, and replay what you missed without doubles. Stack only the pieces you need.
  :::

  #headline
  Audit Logs

  #title
  Compliance-ready :br by composition

  #description
  First-class who-did-what trails as a thin layer on top of wide events. One enricher, one drain wrapper, one helper. Tamper-evident hash chains, denied actions, redact-aware diffs, idempotency keys for safe retries, and typed action catalogs for refactor-safe alerting — all from the main entrypoint, no parallel pipeline.

  #headline
  AI Observability

  #title
  Make AI calls :br observable

  #description
  Your AI endpoints are black boxes. You don't know how many tokens each request burns, which tools the model called, or how fast the stream was. Wrap your model with one line and every call is captured into the wide event. Cost estimation, tool execution timing, streaming performance, cache hits, reasoning tokens, and multi-step agent breakdowns.

  #title
  Add logging, :br not overhead
