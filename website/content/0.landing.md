::landing-features
#body
  :::features-feature-contract
  ---
  link: /learn/the-contract
  link-label: How the contract works
  ---
  #headline
  The Contract

  #title
  One contract. :br Both ends

  #description
  One source of truth for both sides of the wire. Describe your app once on the server, and the client is typed from it automatically — every event, request, channel, and stream, flipped to the client's point of view. Drift between them becomes a red squiggle in your editor, not a 2 a.m. incident.
  :::

  :::features-feature-channels
  ---
  link: /learn/channels
  link-label: How channels work
  ---
  #headline
  Channels

  #title
  Rooms, scoped :br to their members

  #description
  Rooms with membership that actually means something. You decide who gets in, and a broadcast reaches exactly the people inside — the rules live on your server, never shipped to the client to bypass. Presence, online/offline, and dynamic room families come in the box.
  :::

  :::features-feature-streaming
  ---
  link: /learn/streaming
  link-label: How streaming works
  ---
  #headline
  Streaming

  #title
  Push data :br as it's made

  #description
  Send data to the client the moment it exists — token by token, event by event, each chunk typed end to end. Built for LLM output and live feeds, with nudges to steer a run while it's still going and durable resume that picks up right where a disconnect left off.
  :::

  :::features-feature-requests
  ---
  link: /learn/messaging
  link-label: How requests work
  ---
  #headline
  Requests

  #title
  Ask, and :br await the answer

  #description
  Real request/response, over a WebSocket. Ask the server a question and `await` a typed reply — no event wiring, no correlation IDs to track by hand. Fire as many as you like on one connection and each answer comes back to the caller that asked, even out of order. And it works both ways: the server can ask the client too.
  :::

  :::features-feature-reliability
  ---
  link: /learn/reliability
  link-label: How reliability works
  ---
  #headline
  Reliability

  #title
  Disconnected? :br Nothing gets lost

  #description
  Real networks disconnect you — a sleeping laptop, a flaky tower, a deploy mid-session. WSKit keeps the connection healthy and your data consistent through all of it: spot a dead socket, reconnect with backoff, hold the sends you made while offline, and replay what you missed without doubles. Stack only the pieces you need.
  :::

  :::features-feature-tiny
  ---
  link: /start/why-wskit
  link-label: How WSKit stays small
  ---
  #headline
  Opt-in & tiny

  #title
  Small :br by default

  #description
  Small to start, and it stays that way. WSKit is a thin core you build on, not a runtime you adopt — a typed socket weighs about `4.5 KB` gzipped, and every feature is its own side-effect-free module. Add only the primitives this app needs and leave the rest behind, where their code never ships. Turn everything on and you're still around `16 KB`.
  :::

  :::features-feature-anywhere
  ---
  link: /server/adapters/overview
  link-label: How adapters work
  ---
  #headline
  Runs anywhere

  #title
  Your infra, :br any runtime

  #description
  Real-time on infrastructure you own. The same typed handler runs on Cloudflare Durable Objects, Node, Bun, or Deno — swap a single import and your contract, handlers, and client stay exactly the same. No hosted service to rent, no fallback transports, no lock-in.
  :::

  :::features-feature-wall
  #headline
  Batteries included

  #title
  Everything a realtime app needs

  #description
  A socket is the easy part. WSKit brings the pieces you'd otherwise hand-roll — auth, typed errors, scheduling, rate limits, and more — so you build websocket-first features instead of plumbing.
  :::
