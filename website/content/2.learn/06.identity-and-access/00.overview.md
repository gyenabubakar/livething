A socket open to the world is a liability. Real apps need to know *who* is connecting, decide whether to let them in, and control what they can do once they're connected. This final Learn section covers that: authenticating the connection, grouping connections so you can address them, and throttling abuse.

These are mostly server concerns — the server is the gatekeeper — with the client supplying credentials and seeing the consequences.

## The three layers

Think of access control as three layers, from the front door inward:

1. **Authentication** — the front door. The `upgrade` gate runs during the handshake and decides whether a connection is allowed at all. Reject here and the socket never opens.
2. **Tags** — organizing who's connected. Once a connection is in, you can label it — by user, role, team — and then address every connection with a given label as one group.
3. **Rate limiting** — protecting the inside. Even an authenticated user can flood you, so you cap how often events can come in, per connection or per group.
